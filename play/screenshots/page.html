Plain text and the reader

Reading notes · 6 min read

Most text on a phone can be selected but not edited: a web page, a message someone sent you, the description under a video. Selecting a single word is easy with a long press. Selecting exactly the sentence you want, and nothing else, is not.

A selection handle sits under your finger, which hides the very letter you are aiming at. Drag a little too far and the selection jumps to the end of the next word. Drag a little too little and it stays where it was.

Moving by characters and by words

On a desktop the answer is the keyboard: Shift with an arrow key moves the end of the selection by one character, and Control with Shift moves it by one word. The same steps work on any text, whether it can be edited or not.

A pad of buttons on the screen can do the same job. Each press moves the end of the selection one step, reads where it landed, and is ready for the next press. A long press keeps it going until you tap again.

Pages, the start and the end

Longer passages need longer steps. A page step scrolls the text and carries the handle across the whole screen at once, and the start and end buttons run all the way to the edges of the document.

The selection stays the target app's own selection throughout, so copy, share and search work the way they always do once it covers what you wanted.
